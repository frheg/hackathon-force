# Win FORCE by grounding agents in Diskos

The strongest two-day play at the FORCE VibeCode Hackathon (Stavanger, 19-20 November 2026) is not another model or dashboard. It is a **cited, tool-using agent over Diskos well documents and Sodir FactPages, scored on the DISKOS-QA benchmark**, pointed at the problems where NCS money actually leaks: a plugging backlog of about **2,900 wellbores at NOK 50 million to over NOK 1 billion each**, with 78% of the cost paid by the state; well control incidents, **71% of which trace to wrong pore-pressure estimates**; about **91 undeveloped discoveries holding over 500 million Sm3 o.e.**; and field forecasts that over-predict early production by about **20%**. Frontier LLMs already score 97-99.8% on petroleum-geoscience exam questions, so domain knowledge is no longer the bottleneck. Grounded retrieval, extraction from messy scanned reports and honest evaluation are. DISKOS-QA (about 1,027 machine-generated QA pairs over Diskos report passages, built by the FORCE consortium with Sodir) has **no published baseline**, so a team that reports the first one has a result FORCE judges cannot get anywhere else. This report ranks ten apps: the three already built (NCS Copilot, Log Lens, Field Pulse) and seven new concepts. It recommends **Diskos Detective**, a well-dossier agent with a DISKOS-QA scorecard, as the main pitch, demonstrated on a CO2-storage legacy-well question, with the other nine apps presented as skills of one agent that follows a wellbore from prospect to plug. Every concept demos on public data today and swaps in the event's wells, seismic and DiskosQA files through one loader layer.

## The event hands out an evaluation set, not just data

DiskosQA is almost certainly **DISKOS-QA**, an open question-answer benchmark "built from public DISKOS-related oil and gas documents" for domain QA and RAG evaluation ([HF dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/porestar/diskos_qa)). The public copy has **1,027 rows in one split and 16 columns**: the question and answer, the retrieved source passages (`Content`) and their `Chunk_IDs`, the source `Document_name`, list-valued `Well_names`, `Wellbore_names`, `Field_names` and `Formation_names`, a retrieval filter, a `Topic` (rock and core, test fluid and pressure, geology, drilling, petrophysics, VSP), an "evolution" type (reasoning, comparative, multi-context and others), and an **`Entailed` flag** marking whether the answer is supported by its context ([HF dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/porestar/diskos_qa)). A typical row asks what share of GECO Klinkenberg-corrected permeability measurements on 9/2-1 Sandnes Formation cores exceed 100 mD (answer: less than 10%). The pipeline retrieved chunks from a Neo4j knowledge graph of Diskos text linked to wells, fields and formations, generated pairs with Azure OpenAI (**gpt-5-mini** as generator, **o4-mini** as critic, **ada-002** embeddings) and filtered them with DeepEval; the card itself warns the set is "mainly machine-generated and programmatically filtered, not manually annotated" ([GitHub georgeghon/DISKOS-QA](https://github.com/georgeghon/DISKOS-QA)). The repo is NLOD 2.0 and credits FORCE with Equinor, Aker BP, ConocoPhillips, DNO, OMV, Inpex Idemitsu and Sodir. It also ships NER utilities (GLiNER, spaCy), Cypher loaders, raw chunk, well and field CSVs, and a Streamlit feedback app. A LinkedIn post describes "1100 crafted question answer pairs and 50k curated text snippets", which suggests the event package may include the chunk corpus, though that count is unconfirmed in the public repo ([LinkedIn snippet](https://no.linkedin.com/in/dylan-loss-8b541a105)).

The text behind it comes from the **FORCE 2023 hackathon corpus on Zenodo**: 6.1 GB of OCR'd page text, of which **5.7 GB is Diskos reports**, plus UK NSTA, Dutch NLOG and NPD relinquishment reports and 1,500 hand-classified pages separating real text from OCR gibberish, under CC BY 4.0 ([Zenodo 10775273](https://zenodo.org/doi/10.5281/zenodo.10775273)). The 2023 starter pack put the scrubbed corpus at about 755.8M tokens and handed out Azure OpenAI keys at the event ([starter pack](https://github.com/lukasmosser/force_npd_llm_hackathon)). Diskos itself is Norway's national E&P repository, rebuilt as Diskos 2.0 on Halliburton/Landmark's platform for over 28 PB and 79 members ([Oilfield Technology](https://www.oilfieldtechnology.com/exploration/30052023/halliburton-upgrades-norwegian-petroleum-national-data-repository/)). Most well and seismic data becomes public after two years, and interpreted data after five since a 2023 rule change that released about 30,000 datasets at once ([Sodir release of data](https://www.sodir.no/en/facts/data-and-analyses/release-of-data/); [Sodir 2023](https://www.sodir.no/en/whats-new/news/general-news/2023/30000-data-sets-are-now-publicly-accessible)). The most important recent fact for this hackathon: in October 2026 Sodir reported **about 92,000 Diskos well documents converted from PDF to structured JSON**, phase 1 of "several hundred thousand" files due by 2027, covering geology, reservoir properties, drilling events, core analyses, fluid properties and test results. The initiative came from FORCE, and its stated purpose is "AI, analysis tools and digital assistants" ([energy-pedia / Sodir](https://www.energy-pedia.com/news/norway/large-volumes-of-data-from-the-norwegian-offshore-directorate-ready-for-ai-205818)). An app that consumes that JSON is building what the organisers are paying for.

The event page promises "tokens, high-end language models, wells, seismic and DiskosQA data, plus expert mentors", with coding on 19 November, a final sprint on the morning of 20 November and presentations that afternoon ([Sodir event page](https://www.sodir.no/en/force/events/vibecode-hackathon/), via search snippets because the page refuses automated fetches). Microsoft sponsors the tokens ([LinkedIn snippet](https://www.linkedin.com/in/torgeir-stordal-5791a517/)). Judging criteria, team size, prizes, rules on code written in advance and the exact data formats are **not published**, so ask at kickoff. Given the 2023 precedent and the DISKOS-QA tooling, Azure OpenAI is the strong prior, so every app below should talk to an OpenAI-compatible endpoint with the model name in configuration. The table below is the swap-in plan; "likely form" is inference, not confirmed.

Two practical traps recur across every concept. First, **wellbore naming**: the TADI agent paper on Volve had to reconcile three incompatible naming conventions before anything worked ([arXiv 2605.00060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00060)), so a name-normaliser keyed to the FactPages NPDID is day-one infrastructure. Second, **retrieval leakage**: DISKOS-QA ships each question's gold passages in `Content`, so a retriever searching only those passages will look brilliant. An honest score indexes the gold passages inside a larger haystack (the Zenodo Diskos text) and reports results on the `Entailed=True` subset.

## Frontier models know geology; grounding and tools are the open problem

The research record since 2023 points one way. Domain foundation models such as K2 (LLaMA-7B further pre-trained on 5.5B geoscience tokens) and the 30B GeoGalactica were overtaken within two years ([arXiv 2306.05064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05064); [arXiv 2401.00434](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2401.00434v2)). On **FormationEval** (505 petroleum-geoscience MCQs, 72 models, January 2026) Gemini 3 Pro Preview scored **99.8%**, the best open-weight model 98.6% and GPT-4o 92.9%, with **petrophysics the hardest domain for every model** ([arXiv 2601.02158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02158)). Fine-tuning is therefore a poor use of two days. Where models still fail is reading real documents: a LANL study had Llama 2 extract location and depth from 160 historical well records at about **100% accuracy on clean text PDFs but about 70% on scans** ([arXiv 2405.05438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05438)), while a 2026 preprint had a vision-language model read scanned driller's logs directly and extract lithology intervals from **97.2%** of them, with no OCR step ([ESSD preprint](https://essd.copernicus.org/preprints/essd-2026-527/essd-2026-527.pdf)). Structure-aware chunking beat semantic and fixed-size chunking on oil and gas documents at lower cost ([arXiv 2603.24556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24556)). No published benchmark exists for LLM extraction of formation tops, DST results or core descriptions from petroleum well reports, which is a gap a hackathon can partly fill.

The 2025-2026 architecture is the **hybrid agent**: SQL over structured tables, semantic search over reports, and a dozen typed domain tools. TADI is the closest public template. On Equinor's open Volve data it put 12 tables (65,447 rows) in DuckDB and 36,709 documents in ChromaDB, orchestrated 12 tools by function calling, parsed all 1,759 daily drilling reports without error, and did it in 6,084 lines with no framework. Its conclusion was that **tool design matters more than model scale** ([arXiv 2605.00060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00060)). Free-form Text-to-SQL remains fragile: GPT-4 reached 54.89% execution accuracy against 92.96% for humans on BIRD ([arXiv 2503.14251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14251)), and the best oil and gas figure found is 88.5% exact match on 1,711 OSDU-style queries from a fine-tuned T5 in 2023 ([OnePetro ADIPEC 2023](https://onepetro.org/SPEADIP/proceedings-abstract/23ADIP/23ADIP/D021S065R001/534522)). The safe demo design is therefore "typed tools first, SQL fallback". SINTEF's JutulGPT simulator agent learned the same lesson, that one-shot generation performs poorly and an interpret-act-validate loop with linting is what works ([SINTEF blog](https://blog.sintef.com/digital-en/talking-to-your-simulator-what-we-learned-building-jutulgpt/)). On the vendor side, SLB launched the agentic Tela assistant in November 2025 without a time-saving figure ([SLB](https://www.slb.com/news-and-insights/newsroom/press-release/2025/pr-2025-1103-slb-tela-ai)), while Aker BP reports that Cognite agents cut root-cause-analysis engineering time by **more than 70%** ([World Oil](https://worldoil.com/news/2025/9/16/aker-bp-expands-ai-first-strategy-with-cognite-to-transform-e-p-efficiency/)).

The specialist domains each have a mature non-LLM layer that an agent can call rather than replace. In **drilling**, classifying non-productive time from daily-report text is proven: SLB reports nearly **96% accuracy** with a fine-tuned Mistral 7B ([SLB](https://ai.slb.com/blog/extract-drill-risk-genai)), Provectus reports over 94% agreement with experts ([Provectus](https://provectus.com/case-studies/oil-gas-upstream-drilling-npt-genai)), and an EAGE 2026 paper uses multiple agents to pull lessons learned from daily reports with a fact-checking module ([EarthDoc](https://www.earthdoc.org/content/papers/10.3997/2214-4609.2026101276)). In **well logs**, FORCE 2020 remains the reference benchmark and its winners used tuned XGBoost, not deep nets ([GitHub Force-2020](https://github.com/bolgebrygg/Force-2020-Machine-Learning-competition)); WLFM pretrained a well-log foundation model on 1,200 wells but released no weights ([arXiv 2509.18152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18152)). In **seismic**, the shift is to self-supervised foundation models: Sheng et al.'s SFM is MIT-licensed with four downloadable checkpoints ([GitHub](https://github.com/shenghanlin/seismicfoundationmodel)), and the **NCS-Model** family was pretrained on Diskos 3D volumes, beat ImageNet and global seismic baselines on NCS benchmarks, and is pitched for embedding similarity search, with weights described as open-sourced but no repo link found ([arXiv 2603.23211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23211)). VLMs remain weak at geological map and diagram reasoning ([Microsoft Research GeoMap-Bench](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1137041)), so they should explain specialist-model outputs, not interpret seismic alone. In **P&A**, ML cement-log interpretation matched expert bond calls only **51.6%** of the time in one 25-well study ([ResearchGate 2020](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342630351_Automatic_interpretation_of_cement_evaluation_logs_from_cased_boreholes_using_supervised_deep_neural_networks)), while SLB now sells GenAI that turns scanned reports into well barrier schematics with mandatory human review ([SLB 2026](https://www.slb.com/resource-library/article/2026/transforming-plug-and-abandonment-with-wellbarrier--and-generative-ai)). In **production**, the most important NCS paper is not about AI at all: a simple reference-class forecast beat operators' expensive forecasts (see below). The pattern across all five is consistent. Deterministic tools and classic ML produce the numbers; the LLM finds the evidence, calls the tools, reconciles sources and writes the cited brief.

Three readings follow. First, **decommissioning and well integrity are the largest, most public bill**, and because the state pays 78% of P&A through the tax system, Sodir judges hear "NOK saved per plugged well" as taxpayer money. A one-rig-day saving per well at the old NOK 4 million/day planning rate across 2,900 wellbores is about **NOK 11.6bn** (derived from the [SINTEF 2015](https://www.sintef.no/en/latest-news/2015/taking-care-of-elderly-wells/) planning assumptions). Second, **drilling losses are large per event but hard to see in public data**: 35 days of well-control NPT at USD 399k/day is about **USD 14M in rig rate alone** for 2025, or roughly USD 0.9M per incident (derived), before spread costs. Havtil reports no significant reduction in incident counts over a decade ([Havtil RNNP 2024](https://www.havtil.no/en/explore-technical-subjects2/rnnp/rnnp-2024/rnnp-2024-pressemelding)). Third, **decision-quality problems dwarf operational AI savings**. Equinor's AI programme delivered **USD 130M in 2025** and over USD 330M since 2020 ([JPT](https://jpt.spe.org/equinor-says-ai-saved-it-130m-in-2025)), real money but small next to a 20% early-life production shortfall across the NCS, or the roughly 3.1 billion boe in undeveloped discoveries. At the Offshore Norge-implied NOK 670/boe ([Offshore Norge 2026](https://www.offshorenorge.no/en/about-us/news/2026/06/lockout-implemented-production-increasingly-affected)) that is on the order of NOK 2,100bn of gross revenue (derived, not NPV). Two cautions apply. The widely quoted "50-80% of geoscientist time spent finding data" traces back to a 2008 IBM presentation ([Seequent 2011](https://files.seequent.com/PDFs/exploration_information_management_survey_2011.pdf)), so use Seequent's 25% figure instead. McKinsey's USD 230bn upstream AI figure covers all AI, not GenAI ([OGJ](https://www.ogj.com/general-interest/news/55400981/analysis-ai-could-unlock-230-billion-in-annual-upstream-oil-and-gas-value)), so it should not appear on a GenAI slide.

### 1. Diskos Detective: a cited well-dossier agent with the first DISKOS-QA scorecard

**Problem and value.** Geoscientists preparing a prospect review, a P&A programme or a CO2 legacy-well assessment spend days pulling facts from final well reports, core reports and test reports, then cross-checking them against FactPages. Seequent's 2026 survey finds **75% of geoprofessionals spend over a quarter of their time on data management** ([Seequent 2026](https://files.seequent.com/PDFs/TheGeoprofessionalsDataManagementReport_7edition.pdf)), and Sodir is converting several hundred thousand Diskos documents to JSON specifically so assistants can do this work ([energy-pedia / Sodir](https://www.energy-pedia.com/news/norway/large-volumes-of-data-from-the-norwegian-offshore-directorate-ready-for-ai-205818)). Operators pay in geoscientist hours. Sodir and FORCE pay in the conversion programme and want proof it pays back. There is **no public hours-saved figure for a subsurface document assistant at any operator**, so the honest value claim is twofold. The app makes decisions on wells costing USD 36-42M ([Westwood](https://www.westwoodenergy.com/news/westwood-insight/westwood-insight-norway-state-of-exploration-2014-2023)) faster and better evidenced. And the demo itself measures minutes-per-dossier against a manual baseline, which is a number the industry does not have. Aker BP's 70% time cut from root-cause agents is the closest analogue ([World Oil](https://worldoil.com/news/2025/9/16/aker-bp-expands-ai-first-strategy-with-cognite-to-transform-e-p-efficiency/)).

**Data and technique.** The structured side is the existing DuckDB of FactPages (wellbores, formation tops, cores, DSTs, mud, coordinates, discoveries, fields), extended with the standardised Open Well Data tables on Zenodo ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/24270272)). The text side is the DISKOS-QA passages embedded inside the Zenodo Diskos corpus (or the event's chunk set and Sodir JSON if provided), indexed in Qdrant with metadata filters on well, wellbore, field and formation. The agent follows TADI's design ([arXiv 2605.00060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00060)), with typed tools such as `wellbore_header`, `formation_tops`, `dst_results`, `search_reports(query, wellbore, formation)` and `nearby_wellbores`, and answers only with citations to `Document_name` and chunk. Retrieval uses structure-aware chunks ([arXiv 2603.24556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24556)) and hybrid keyword plus embedding search, because well names and formation names are exact strings that embeddings blur. The scorecard runs four configurations on the `Entailed=True` subset: closed-book, plain RAG, entity-filtered RAG (the KG idea DISKOS-QA itself was built on) and the full agent. It reports answer correctness by LLM judge, faithfulness, and citation precision (did the cited chunk ID match the gold `Chunk_IDs`), broken down by `Topic` and `Evolutions` type. GAIA's 3.1x RAGAS gain over a base model is a published reference point for the size of the effect ([arXiv 2511.03852](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03852)).

**Why it wins and what can break.** It is the only concept that turns the event's headline dataset into a **number nobody has published**, on a benchmark built by the judges' own consortium, and it delivers the "digital assistant" Sodir's JSON programme promises. The main risks are benchmark artefacts. The answers are synthetic and generated by gpt-5-mini, so a GPT-family model may score well on style rather than truth; mitigate by also judging with a different model family and spot-checking 30 rows by hand with a mentor. Closed-book answerability is the second trap: if the frontier model answers many questions without retrieval, report it, because that is itself a finding about the benchmark. Token budget is the third, since four configurations across 1,027 questions with a judge is several thousand calls; run the full grid on a stratified 200-question sample and the best configuration on the rest.

**Two-day build.** Before the event, if rules allow: download DISKOS-QA and a filtered slice of the Zenodo Diskos text (the 5.7 GB file is too big to embed whole; take the documents named in `Document_name` plus a few thousand random distractor documents), and build the wellbore-name normaliser. Day 1 morning: index the corpus, wire the five typed tools to DuckDB and Qdrant, and get one end-to-end cited answer. Day 1 afternoon: the evaluation harness and the 200-question grid running in the background; meanwhile build the dossier view (pick a wellbore on the map, get a one-page cited summary of tops, shows, cores, DSTs and drilling problems). Evening: swap in the event's DiskosQA files and reports. Day 2 morning: the scorecard chart by topic, a timed manual-vs-agent run on one wellbore with a mentor, and the CO2 legacy-well demo question that hands over to Barrier Ledger.

### 2. Barrier Ledger: legacy-well dossiers for P&A and CO2 storage from one extraction

**Problem and value.** The same per-well facts (casing shoes, cement tops, plug depths, caprock and creeping-shale intervals, abandonment status) decide two expensive questions. How should this well be permanently plugged under NORSOK D-010, and can CO2 be stored next to it? On the P&A side, about **2,900 wellbores** await plugging at **NOK 50M to over NOK 1bn** each, only 30-40 are plugged a year against 100-150 new production wells, and the state pays **78%** ([Sodir via energy-pedia](https://www.energy-pedia.com/news/norway/new-technology-is-profitable-when-shutting-down-wells-201921)). Havtil uses about 35 days per permanent P&A as a planning benchmark, against a best case of about 10 ([Havtil 2025 presentation](https://events.havtil.no/hubfs/Arrangement%20-%20delte%20ressurser/Fagdag%20om%20plugging%20og%20forlating/Havtil%20presentation%207th%20of%20May.pdf?hsLang=en)). Operators name barrier evaluation, overburden barriers and dual-string logging as their top technical needs ([Offshore Norge P&A outlook](https://www.offshorenorge.no/contentassets/de2f08a3fd4f4796bc00d05dde457e34/plug-and-abadonment-outlook-ncs-2023---2028.pdf)). On the CCS side, Norwegian regulation requires a risk assessment of every relevant legacy well ([DNV, FORCE 2025](https://www.sodir.no/4add82/globalassets/2-force/2025/legacy-wells/dnv---legacy-well-assessment-through-the-lens-of-best-pratice-guidelines-and-standards.pdf)). Emmel et al. found that **three published screening methods classify the same offshore Norway wells systematically differently**, and that **no machine-readable P&A records database exists** ([Wiley GHG 2025](https://scijournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ghg.70045?af=R)). A wrong "intervene" call costs about **GBP 25-30M** per well ([UK Gov](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7c6b8940f0b626628abee6/CO2_Storage_Liabilities_in_the_North_Sea_-_An_Assessment_of_Risks_and_Fi....pdf)). Northern Lights' permit shows what a good answer looks like: four of five relevant legacy wells had two effective barriers, and the fifth was judged unreachable by the plume ([Offshore Norge, FORCE 2025](https://www.sodir.no/4add7a/globalassets/2-force/2025/legacy-wells/offshore-norge---when-will-legacy-well-leakage-risk-in-co2-storage-sites-require-well-intervention.pdf)).

**Data and technique.** Public today: FactPages wellbore headers, status, dates, formation tops and the casing and leak-off test data exposed among the wellbore attributes ([FactPages attributes](https://factpages.sodir.no/en/wellbore/attributes)); CO2 storage licence outlines from FactMaps; the Smeaheia dataset with well reports, seismic and a geomodel ([CO2DataShare](https://co2datashare.org/dataset/smeaheia-dataset)); and the Diskos report text in the Zenodo corpus for cement and plug details. The technique is the Diskos Detective retriever plus a schema-constrained extractor (casing string, shoe depth, cement top, plug interval, each with page citation), VLM-first on scanned pages given the 100% vs 70% text-vs-scan gap ([arXiv 2405.05438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05438)). Deterministic rules then derive candidate barrier depths from formation tops (caprock and shale intervals), and a scoring layer runs each well through two or three published screening schemes and shows where they disagree and which missing fact drives the disagreement. That last view is new per Emmel et al. The commercial benchmark is SLB's GenAI Wellbarrier, which chains OCR, NLP and LLMs into schematics with human review ([SLB 2026](https://www.slb.com/resource-library/article/2026/transforming-plug-and-abandonment-with-wellbarrier--and-generative-ai)). The UK Southern North Sea study that ranked 2,284 wells into four categories gives a published scheme to copy ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666759225000964)).

**Why it wins and what can break.** It speaks directly to taxpayer money, to Havtil's TA-well concerns and to the CCS legacy-wells seminar FORCE ran in November 2025 ([FORCE seminar](https://www.sodir.no/en/force/news/2025/in-person-ccs-legacy-wells-seminar)), and its output is a dossier a regulator can audit line by line. The hard limits: the D-010 acceptance tables are paywalled, so the app must say "candidate barrier, check against EAC tables" rather than certify anything. Casing and cement data for many wells sit only in reports that may not be in the public corpus, so coverage will be patchy; show the data-gap checklist as a feature, not a failure. And screening schemes from the literature must be transcribed carefully, which is a good job for a mentor.

**Two-day build.** Day 1 morning: select the wellbores inside or near the Smeaheia and Northern Lights outlines from FactMaps (a few dozen), pull their FactPages records, and find their reports in the corpus. Day 1 afternoon: the extraction schema, prompts and a hand-checked sample of ten wells for precision. Evening: two screening schemes as code, plus the disagreement matrix. Day 2 morning: the well card (a draft barrier sketch drawn from extracted depths, candidate barrier formations, missing data, risk class under each scheme, every line cited) and a map coloured by agreement. Swap the event's well reports in wherever they cover these wellbores.

### 3. Spud Brief: a pre-spud offset-well hazard brief in Havtil's own format

**Problem and value.** Havtil attributes **71% of NCS well-control incidents in 2013-2022 to erroneous or too-low pore-pressure estimates**. Its recommendations are concrete: present predictions by measured depth with lower and upper bounds rather than P95 labels, share depletion and injection pressures across licences, and adopt standard plots and a checklist building on IOGP RP 608 ([JPT 2026](https://jpt.spe.org/study-highlights-link-between-pore-pressure-uncertainty-and-well-control-incidents)). Shallow gas and shallow water flow in top holes and pilot holes are "a significant share" of incidents ([Havtil 2025 shallow hazards study](https://www.havtil.no/en/explore-technical-subjects2/technical-competence/reports-from-projects/2025/challenges-and-opportunities-for-shallow-hazard-risk-reduction/)). In 2025, 15 incidents cost **35 days of NPT** ([JPT 2026](https://jpt.spe.org/study-highlights-link-between-pore-pressure-uncertainty-and-well-control-incidents)), about USD 14M in rig rate at USD 399k/day (derived). Operators and drilling contractors carry the cost, and the regulator owns the problem statement.

**Data and technique.** Given a planned location, the app selects offset wellbores by distance and stratigraphic similarity from FactPages, then assembles their mud weights (Open Well Data lists about 36,752 mud rows), LOT/FIT results, DSTs, formation tops and the wellbore history text ([Zenodo Open Well Data](https://zenodo.org/records/24097890); [FactMaps changelog listing `wellbore_history`](https://www.sodir.no/49bc11/globalassets/1-sodir/om-oss/informasjonstjenester/karttjenester/factmaps_3_changelog.pdf)). An LLM extracts hazard mentions (shallow gas, kicks, losses, overpressure, tight hole, H2S) with depth and formation from the history text, the Diskos report corpus and DISKOS-QA's drilling and test-pressure topics, each with a citation. The output is a depth-indexed chart with the offset mud-weight envelope, LOT points and hazard markers, plus a one-page brief laid out as Havtil recommends. The method follows the EAGE 2026 multi-agent lessons-learned workflow with fact-checking ([EarthDoc](https://www.earthdoc.org/content/papers/10.3997/2214-4609.2026101276)) and the Apache/Intellicess RAG over more than 200 wells that cites its sources and declines when it lacks an answer ([Drilling Contractor](https://drillingcontractor.org/ai-enabled-large-language-model-speeds-up-wells-data-retrieval-but-must-be-used-with-care-69503)). Quantitative pore-pressure modelling (Eaton from logs and velocities) stays out of scope.

**Why it wins and what can break.** It turns a regulator's published finding into a working tool in the regulator's own building, and no public NCS-wide offset-hazard product was found. The `wellbore_history` columns were not verified, so check the DataService metadata on day one and fall back to scraping the FactPages wellbore page text if needed. Mud weight is a proxy for pore pressure, not a measurement, and the brief must say so. Drilling engineers will rightly distrust anything that looks like a prediction, so frame it as an evidence pack for the engineer.

**Two-day build.** Day 1 morning: the offset selector (spatial kNN plus shared formations) and the mud, LOT and DST joins. Day 1 afternoon: hazard extraction over history text for a test area such as Haltenbanken, where Havtil documented pilot-hole incidents ([Havtil lessons learned Haltenbanken](https://www.havtil.no/contentassets/c8fd98ea9d4b4a609e44393bc094b779/lessons-learned-and-strategy-from-haltenbanken.pdf)), and the depth chart. Day 2 morning: the brief generator, then a backtest on a recent well with a known event: hide it, generate the brief from older offsets, and show whether the hazard was flagged.

### 4. NCS Copilot (baseline): keep it, but put typed tools in front of the SQL

NCS Copilot already answers natural-language questions over FactPages with a map, which is the expected "first app" at this event and a solid tool for every other concept. Its weakness is the generic one: free Text-to-SQL is far less reliable than it looks in a live demo (54.89% execution accuracy for GPT-4 on BIRD, [arXiv 2503.14251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14251)), and the best oil and gas figure is 88.5% from a fine-tuned model ([OnePetro](https://onepetro.org/SPEADIP/proceedings-abstract/23ADIP/23ADIP/D021S065R001/534522)). The two-day upgrade is a 50-100 question gold set with execution accuracy reported on a slide, typed tools for the 15 most common question shapes with SQL as fallback, and the event's tables loaded under the same read-only path. Its value case is the data-time figure above. Its role in the story is the structured-facts layer that Diskos Detective calls.

### 5. Tie-back Matchmaker: rank stranded discoveries against the hosts that are about to close

**Problem and value.** At end-2025 Sodir counted about **91 discoveries awaiting a development decision with over 500 million Sm3 o.e.**, mostly small North Sea finds that are only profitable as tie-backs ([Sodir RR2026](https://www.sodir.no/en/whats-new/publications/reports/resource-report/resource-report-2026/summary/)). Of 82 undeveloped gas discoveries in 2024, extraction was judged highly unlikely for 47 ([Sodir RR2024](https://sodir.no/globalassets/1-sodir/publikasjoner/rapporter/ressursrapporter/2024/en/resource-report-2024.pdf)). Sodir's 2026 FORCE event states the problem: the economic window for tie-ins **narrows as host fields mature**, and decisions are time-critical ([FORCE abstract](https://www.sodir.no/en/force/events/in-person-unlocking-the-potential/abstract)). The 2025 discoveries averaged only about 3.2 million Sm3 o.e. each (67 across 21 finds, derived from [norskpetroleum.no](https://www.norskpetroleum.no/en/exploration/exploration-activity/)), so commerciality, not geological success, is the binding constraint. Licensees and the state both lose when a host ceases first. The undeveloped portfolio is roughly NOK 2,100bn of gross revenue at NOK 670/boe (derived, not NPV).

**Data and technique.** Everything is public now: FactPages discoveries (location, status, fluid, resource estimate), fields, monthly field production, field reserves and descriptions, facilities and pipelines from FactMaps. The scorer ranks each discovery-host pair by distance, fluid compatibility, host decline from peak as a proxy for spare capacity, and remaining host life from reserves over current rate. A **reference-class production profile** for the discovery, scaled from abandoned NCS fields as in Nesvold and Bratvold ([Sodir FORCE PDF](https://www.sodir.no/globalassets/2-force/2023/dokumenter/debiasing-production-forecasts_published-version.pdf)), shows whether the discovery's plateau fits inside the host's remaining window. The LLM writes a one-page screening memo citing the discovery and field descriptions. The area-development logic follows Sodir's Trell and Trine and Norne fact boxes ([Trell and Trine](https://sodir.no/en/whats-new/publications/reports/resource-report/resource-report-2022/3-competition-and-value-creation/fact-box--trell-and-trine)).

**Why it wins and what can break.** It addresses a problem Sodir itself is campaigning on, it is fully buildable on public data, and it reuses Field Pulse's decline machinery, so day one starts half done. Host ullage is not published, and decline from peak is a crude proxy; fluid compatibility and processing constraints are simplified; and there are no economics beyond distance and timing. Present it as a screening radar that tells a licence team which three pairs deserve a real study.

**Two-day build.** Day 1: discovery and host tables, the distance and decline scoring, and the map with ranked lines. Day 2 morning: the reference-class overlay and the memo generator, demonstrated on one named cluster. Event wells near a discovery add evidence to its memo through Diskos Detective.

### 6. Lost Time Hunter: invisible lost time and lessons learned from daily drilling reports

**Problem and value.** Daily drilling reports hide non-productive time inside "productive" lines. A CSIRO study flagged **923 hours** of potential NPT among observations logged as normal ([APPEA Journal](https://www.publish.csiro.au/AJ/fulltext/AJ21118)), and Halliburton defines invisible lost time as actual duration minus a technical-limit target ([Halliburton](https://www.halliburton.com/en/resources/investigating-invisible-lost-time-on-textual-data-using-artificial-intelligence)). Provectus reports AI-discovered NPT equal to **7% of reported NPT**, USD 42k per US onshore well and USD 4.2M over a 100-well campaign (vendor claim) ([Provectus](https://provectus.com/case-studies/oil-gas-upstream-drilling-npt-genai)). On an NCS semi-sub at USD 399k/day, every recovered day is worth about USD 0.4M in rig rate alone (derived). Operators pay the rig; drilling contractors carry the reputational cost of NPT.

**Data and technique.** Volve's **1,759 daily drilling reports** are available as an LLM-ready Hugging Face dataset ([HF volve_alpaca](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bengsoon/volve_alpaca/raw/main/README.md)), and TADI parsed the original WITSML without errors ([arXiv 2605.00060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00060)). An LLM extracts each activity line into a schema (operation class, hole section, depth, duration, NPT flag, cause from a 13-category taxonomy like Provectus's, free-text root cause), validated against a hand-labelled sample of about 100 lines. Invisible lost time is the gap between each operation's duration and a percentile benchmark for its class and hole size, using percentiles rather than a composite best as in SPE 188238 ([SLB](https://slb.com/resource-library/technical-paper/so/spe-188238)). A lessons-learned RAG then answers "what went wrong drilling the 12¼-inch section through the Hugin" with citations. The literature lineage runs from EVENT/SYMPTOM/ACTION sentence mining on 9,670 reports ([arXiv 1712.01476](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.01476)) to SLB's 96%-accurate fine-tuned classifier.

**Why it wins and what can break.** The pattern is proven, so the demo will work, and the money per day is easy to grasp. It ranks only sixth because Volve is **one field from 2008-2016**, the licence is Equinor's own open-data licence rather than fully open ([Agile Scientific](https://agilescientific.com/blog/2018/10/16/volve-not-open-after-all)), and vendors (SLB, Halliburton, Exebenus) already occupy the space. It becomes much stronger if the event or a team member's company can supply DDRs from more wells: a drilling contractor's own rig reports would drop straight into the same schema.

**Two-day build.** Day 1: load the reports, the extraction prompt, and the labelled sample with an accuracy number. Day 2 morning: the per-well time-depth chart with NPT and ILT shaded, the ranking of causes in rig-days and USD, and the lessons-learned chat. Feed its cause catalogue into Spud Brief as offset evidence.

### 7. Field Pulse (baseline): add the outside view that beat the operators

Field Pulse forecasts field decline against Sodir remaining reserves and writes an LLM field brief. The research makes its upgrade obvious. On 71 NCS oil fields, operators over-predicted early production by about **20%**, almost 70% of fields under-delivered after year four, outcomes fell below the P10 low case almost 60% of the time after year two, and a **reference-class forecast built only from abandoned fields' curves "by far outperforms"** the industry's methods ([Nesvold and Bratvold 2022](https://www.sodir.no/globalassets/2-force/2023/dokumenter/debiasing-production-forecasts_published-version.pdf)). RNB field forecasts are not public, so Field Pulse cannot score operator forecasts directly. It can show the outside-view band next to each field's decline fit, and track how FactPages reserve estimates for each field have moved over time. A transformer forecaster would be a weaker pitch on monthly field-level data, given that result. The value anchor is derived: 1% of Equinor's roughly 1.2 million boe/d at NOK 670/boe is about NOK 2.9bn a year ([Equinor](https://www.equinor.com/news/20260107-artificial-intelligence-saved-equinor-usd-130-million); [Offshore Norge](https://www.offshorenorge.no/en/about-us/news/2026/06/lockout-implemented-production-increasingly-affected)). Johan Sverdrup's NOK 2bn+ first-year gain from digital optimisation is the sourced NCS precedent ([Equinor](https://www.equinor.com/en/news/20201005-johan-sverdrup-first-year.html)). The upgrade costs about half a day, and the reference-class code is shared with Tie-back Matchmaker.

### 8. Data Doctor: a cross-source consistency auditor for NCS well data

**Problem and value.** Every app above inherits the errors in its inputs. Total depth, kelly-bushing elevation, spud date, coordinates, formation tops and DST intervals appear in FactPages, LAS headers and report text, and they do not always agree. Published ML QC works on log signals (outlier detection, depth matching) ([SLB SPE-202977](https://www.slb.com/resource-library/technical-paper/di/spe-202977-ms)), not on cross-document consistency, and raster-log digitisation is still weak, with VeerNet at 35% F1 ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10381386/)). The payer is the data manager at every operator and Sodir as Diskos administrator, which is converting hundreds of thousands of documents and needs a way to check them. The money case is indirect, through the 25%-of-time figure and through every downstream decision, which is why it ranks eighth despite being easy to build.

**Data and technique.** FactPages headers and tops as one source, FORCE 2020 LAS headers and the curves' own depth ranges as a second ([Zenodo 4351156](https://zenodo.org/records/4351156)), and LLM or VLM extraction from report text as a third. Rules diff the values with tolerances and flag conflicts with citations. Precision is measured on a hand-labelled sample. Run the same extraction on a clean text page and a scanned page to show the 100% vs 70% gap ([arXiv 2405.05438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05438)) and whether VLM-first reading closes it ([ESSD preprint](https://essd.copernicus.org/preprints/essd-2026-527/essd-2026-527.pdf)).

**Why it wins and what can break.** Judges who run national data repositories care about trust, and a conflict list with citations is concrete and auditable. The risk is a dull demo, and "ground truth" that is itself wrong; present conflicts as questions for a human, not as errors. **Build:** day 1 extraction and diff on the 118 FORCE 2020 wells; day 2 morning a conflict dashboard and a "trust badge" that the other apps display next to each fact.

### 9. Log Lens (baseline): wrap it as a tool and swap in the event's logs

Log Lens trains FORCE 2020 lithology ML and has an LLM explain intervals. It sits on the canonical NCS benchmark, where tuned XGBoost won and the top five to seven entries were statistically tied ([GitHub Force-2020](https://github.com/bolgebrygg/Force-2020-Machine-Learning-competition); [winner write-up](https://ibrahim-olawale13.medium.com/force-2020-machine-learning-lithology-predictionwinning-solution-8cbf78290b41)), which makes it reliable but not novel. The evidence supports two upgrades. Petrophysics is where LLMs are weakest ([arXiv 2601.02158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02158)), so Vsh, porosity and Archie water saturation should be computed by open libraries (petropy, stoneforge) and only explained by the LLM. And predicted lithology can be checked against report descriptions retrieved by Diskos Detective, showing where model, log and geologist disagree. A recommender-style top predictor from Sodir's published tops is a cheap extra ([arXiv 2202.08869](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08869)). The event's LAS files drop in through lasio once curve mnemonics are mapped to the FORCE 2020 names.

### 10. Seismic Lookalike: click a feature, find its twins, explain them with wells

**Problem and value.** Manual structural interpretation takes weeks to months ([USPTO 11320551](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11320551)). SLB reports ML cut top-of-salt picking from **400 to 144 hours** (vendor) ([SLB](https://www.slb.com/resource-library/case-study-with-navigation/di/accelerating-seismic-interpretation-in-the-kwanza-basin)), and Equinor says AI let it interpret 2 million km² of seismic in 2025, **ten times faster** ([JPT](https://jpt.spe.org/equinor-says-ai-saved-it-130m-in-2025)). With NCS exploration wells at USD 36-42M and a 28% commercial success rate, better analogue finding has obvious value, but no published NCS ROI exists.

**Data and technique.** Embed seismic patches with SFM (MIT, weights available) or the NCS-Model if its weights can be located, index them in Qdrant, and let a user click a feature on an inline to find similar patches across the survey, as the NCS-Model authors propose ([arXiv 2603.23211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23211)). Add pretrained FaultSeg3D overlays on 128³ tiles ([GitHub faultSeg](https://www.github.com/xinwucwp/faultSeg)) and zero-shot SAM with point prompts, which its authors conclude works best as an interpreter's assistant ([arXiv 2606.15786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15786)). The LLM agent explains each match using the nearest wellbores' tops and Diskos Detective's report evidence; it never interprets seismic images alone, given VLMs' documented weakness at geological visual reasoning ([GeoMap-Bench](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1137041)). Public stand-ins today are Volve, the Smeaheia 3D seismic and F3 Netherlands.

**Why it wins and what can break.** It is the flashiest demo and the one that uses the event's seismic most directly, but it is the riskiest: GPU access, SEG-Y handling, CUDA versions for SFM, the unlocated NCS-Model weights and the absence of any accuracy measure in two days. Only 5 of 80 registered teams in the FORCE 2020 fault competition submitted a valid result ([GitHub Force-2020](https://github.com/bolgebrygg/Force-2020-Machine-Learning-competition)). **Build:** day 1 load and tile the survey and embed one inline range; day 2 morning similarity search UI, fault overlay and the well-explained match. Attempt it only with a team member who has run seismic deep learning before.

## Pitch Diskos Detective and tell one wellbore's life story

The recommended main pitch is **Diskos Detective**, presented in three beats. The first is the number: "first public DISKOS-QA baseline", with plain RAG versus entity-filtered RAG versus agent, broken down by topic, on a slide FORCE has never seen. The second is the minutes: a timed dossier for one wellbore, manual against agent, with every line cited back to a Diskos document. The third is the money: the agent answering a CO2 legacy-well question for a Smeaheia or Northern Lights wellbore through the Barrier Ledger view, which ties the demo to a GBP 25-30M intervention decision and to the NOK 800bn plugging bill that the state pays 78% of. This combination beats leading with Barrier Ledger alone because the scorecard is guaranteed to exist by Friday morning even if casing and cement extraction coverage is thin, and it beats leading with Seismic Lookalike because it cannot fail on GPU logistics. If the event's DiskosQA package turns out to include the chunk corpus or Sodir JSON, the pitch gets stronger without any change in story; if it turns out to be only the public CSV, the haystack method still produces an honest number.

The ten apps then become **one agent with ten skills** sharing one evidence layer: the FactPages DuckDB, the Diskos text index, the wellbore-name normaliser and Data Doctor's trust badges. The narrative follows a single wellbore through its life, which is easy for a mixed audience of geoscientists, engineers and IT people to follow. Seismic Lookalike finds the prospect's analogues. Tie-back Matchmaker asks whether a discovery there would have a host in time. Spud Brief warns the drilling team what the offsets met. Lost Time Hunter learns from what the rig actually did. Log Lens and NCS Copilot characterise what was found. Field Pulse checks whether the production forecast is realistic. Barrier Ledger plans how the well is plugged, or whether CO2 can be stored beside it. Diskos Detective is the engine underneath every step, and its DISKOS-QA score is the reason to trust any of them. In practice the team should build the shared layer and Diskos Detective first, give one person each to Barrier Ledger and Spud Brief, re-wrap the three existing apps as tools on the morning of day 2, and show the rest as roadmap slides unless spare capacity appears.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "ambitious" should mean at this event. Model capability is no longer scarce: frontier models pass petroleum-geoscience exams at near-perfect rates, and FORCE is handing out the tokens. What is scarce is **evidence**, meaning grounded answers, measured accuracy and cited sources a regulator can audit. That makes a measured result on DISKOS-QA a more durable contribution than any single clever interface, and it explains why every high-value concept here (plugging, legacy wells, pore-pressure hazards, tie-backs) reduces to the same capability of reading Diskos reports, reconciling them with FactPages and showing the receipts. The larger NCS money is in decisions (which well to plug how, which discovery to tie back before its host closes, which forecast to believe) rather than in operational speedups. Equinor's USD 130M a year of AI value is small next to a 20% forecast shortfall or a NOK 800bn plugging bill.

Two uncertainties deserve attention at kickoff rather than after. Whether the event's DiskosQA includes the 50k chunks or Sodir's JSON documents determines whether the agent works on real Diskos structure or on OCR'd text. And whether code prepared in advance is allowed determines how much of the shared layer can be ready on Thursday morning. Both should be asked in the first hour. Beyond the event, a team that leaves Stavanger with a public DISKOS-QA leaderboard and a reusable NCS evidence layer has something Sodir's 2027 JSON completion will make more valuable each month, which is a better outcome than a prize.
